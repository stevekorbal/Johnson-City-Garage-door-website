A garage door that suddenly refuses to open can turn into a major inconvenience—especially when your vehicle is trapped inside. Sometimes the problem is as simple as a dead remote battery, while other situations can involve a broken spring, damaged cable, opener failure, or track problem.

If your **garage door won't open in Johnson City, TN**, here are 7 common causes to check and signs that it may be time to request professional garage door repair.

---

## 1. The Garage Door Spring Is Broken

A broken garage door spring is one of the most common reasons a door suddenly becomes difficult or impossible to open.

Garage door springs counterbalance much of the door's weight. When a spring breaks, the opener may struggle to lift the door or the door may only rise a few inches.

Common warning signs include:

- A loud bang coming from the garage
- A visible gap in the torsion spring
- The garage door only opening a few inches
- The opener running but struggling to lift the door
- A door that suddenly feels extremely heavy

Garage door springs operate under significant tension. Avoid attempting to adjust or replace a broken spring yourself.

---

## 2. The Garage Door Opener Isn't Working

Sometimes the garage door itself isn't the problem. The issue may be with the automatic opener.

Start by checking whether the opener has electrical power.

You can also try operating the door using the wall-mounted control instead of the remote.

If the wall button works but the remote doesn't, the problem could be:

- Dead remote batteries
- Remote programming
- Signal interference
- A damaged opener antenna
- Receiver problems

If neither the remote nor wall control operates the opener, the motor, electronics, wiring, or power supply may need further diagnosis.

---

## 3. The Safety Sensors Are Blocked or Misaligned

Garage door openers use photo-eye safety sensors positioned near the bottom of the tracks.

If the sensors become dirty, blocked, damaged, or misaligned, they can interfere with normal garage door operation.

Check for:

- Dirt on the sensor lenses
- Objects blocking the sensor beam
- Misaligned sensors
- Loose or damaged wires
- Flashing sensor indicator lights

You can carefully clean the lenses with a soft cloth and remove anything blocking the sensors.

If the problem continues, the sensors or wiring may require service.

---

## 4. The Garage Door Has Come Off Track

Garage door rollers are designed to move smoothly through the metal tracks on each side of the door.

If a roller comes out of the track or a track becomes bent or damaged, the garage door may become stuck.

Signs of a track problem can include:

- The garage door sitting crooked
- Rollers outside the track
- Bent track sections
- Grinding or scraping noises
- Uneven movement
- The door becoming stuck partway

If the door appears crooked or off-track, stop operating it.

Continuing to use the opener could cause additional damage to the door, rollers, cables, tracks, or opener.

---

## 5. A Garage Door Cable Is Damaged

Garage door cables work with the spring system to help safely raise and lower the door.

A damaged cable may be:

- Frayed
- Loose
- Hanging beside the door
- Wrapped incorrectly around a drum
- Completely broken

You may also notice one side of the garage door sitting lower than the other.

Because garage door cables interact with components under significant tension, cable repairs should generally be handled by a trained garage door technician.

---

## 6. The Garage Door Is Manually Locked

Before assuming you have a major mechanical problem, check the garage door's manual lock.

Some garage doors have locking mechanisms that prevent the door from opening even when the automatic opener is activated.

Also check whether your opener has been placed into a vacation or lock mode that disables remote controls.

If the door is locked, repeatedly operating the opener can place unnecessary stress on the system.

---

## 7. Worn Rollers, Hinges, or Other Hardware Are Causing the Door to Bind

Your garage door contains numerous moving components that must work together smoothly.

These include:

- Rollers
- Hinges
- Tracks
- Bearings
- Springs
- Cables
- Brackets

Worn or damaged hardware can cause the garage door to hesitate, bind, shake, or stop during operation.

If the door repeatedly gets stuck at the same point, don't keep forcing it with the opener.

The underlying mechanical problem should be identified before additional components become damaged.

## What Can You Safely Check Yourself?

Before requesting professional service, there are several basic things homeowners can safely check.

Try:

- Checking whether the opener has power
- Replacing the remote batteries
- Trying the wall-mounted control
- Checking for objects blocking the door
- Cleaning the safety sensor lenses
- Making sure the door isn't manually locked
- Looking for obvious damage without touching springs or cables

If these basic checks don't solve the problem, there may be a mechanical or electrical issue requiring professional diagnosis.

---

## Don't Keep Running the Garage Door Opener

One of the biggest mistakes homeowners can make is repeatedly pressing the opener when the garage door refuses to move.

Your garage door opener isn't designed to compensate for a failed spring or serious mechanical problem.

Continuing to operate the system could place unnecessary strain on the opener and other components.

If your garage door isn't moving normally, stop operating it until the problem can be identified.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why won't my garage door open even though the motor is running?

A running opener doesn't necessarily mean the opener itself is working correctly. A broken spring, disconnected trolley, damaged cable, stripped opener component, or another mechanical problem could prevent the door from moving.

### Why does my garage door only open a few inches?

A broken spring is one possible cause. Other potential problems include binding hardware, track issues, opener settings, or other mechanical problems.

### Can I manually open a garage door with a broken spring?

A garage door with a broken spring can be extremely heavy and difficult to control. Attempting to lift it can create a safety risk. Professional service is recommended when spring failure is suspected.

### Should I keep pressing the opener if the garage door won't open?

No. Repeatedly operating the opener when the door is stuck can place additional strain on the opener and garage door components. Stop operating the system until the cause of the problem is identified.

### When should I call for garage door repair?

If basic checks such as replacing remote batteries, checking power, cleaning the safety sensors, and checking the manual lock don't solve the problem, professional diagnosis may be appropriate.
